# P09.4 Protected-Readiness Review

## Status

**DEVELOPMENT MECHANICS QUALIFIED — PROTECTED P09.4 WORK NOT YET AUTHORIZED**

This review evaluates whether the frozen P09.4 development evidence is mature
enough to proceed from implementation qualification into protected scientific
preregistration.

It does not generate a protected C1 seed.

It does not generate a protected population.

It does not authorize protected treatment execution.

It does not establish H1 support or C3 superiority.

---

## Frozen development evidence reviewed

The reviewed chain includes:

- scientific C0-C3 arm-semantics preregistration;
- resource-only scientific budget selection;
- scientific-arm implementation;
- complete F1-F5 plus out-of-RFS development-matrix preregistration;
- pre-execution lifecycle-scope clarification;
- first-observed development qualification result.

The development matrix contained exactly 24 primary arm runs and an exact
24-run deterministic repeat.

The qualification contract passed with zero frozen violations.

---

## What is now established

The development evidence establishes that the frozen implementation can
mechanically execute the intended four-arm comparison under budget 13.

Within the frozen deterministic development environment:

- C0 remains trace-only;
- C1 follows deterministic caller-supplied seeded-random selection;
- C2 follows maximum expected information gain;
- C3 follows information gain per calibrated replay cost;
- observations come from executed checkpoint replay;
- calibrated replay cost is physically accounted;
- experiment consumption is enforced;
- confirmation consumes ordinary replay cost;
- budget exhaustion remains observable;
- shared open-set terminal semantics remain active;
- and the out-of-RFS control does not produce a specific RFS diagnosis.

This is implementation and mechanism qualification.

It is not protected scientific evidence.

---

## Binding-budget observation

Budget 13 is operationally binding in development.

Some frozen development arm runs terminated `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

This is not treated as an implementation defect because budget exhaustion was
preregistered as a legitimate observable treatment outcome.

The protected protocol must preserve budget exhaustion in the denominator.

The protected design must not increase the budget merely because a development
arm exhausted it.

## Protected-readiness decision

**PROCEED TO PROTECTED SCIENTIFIC PREREGISTRATION DESIGN ONLY.**

Development mechanics are sufficiently qualified to justify freezing the
remaining protected-study protocol.

Protected generation and protected execution remain blocked.

---

## Remaining gate 1 — protected master protocol

Before any protected seed or protected case generation, freeze a single master
protected-study protocol.

It must define at minimum:

- protected study version;
- exact scientific question;
- exact primary and secondary comparisons;
- protected in-RFS strata;
- protected out-of-RFS safety stratum;
- sample counts per stratum;
- total protected sample count;
- case-ID contract;
- treatment-visible representation;
- hidden-truth representation;
- development/protected isolation;
- protected case-generation procedure;
- protected generation randomness procedure;
- duplicate-case prevention;
- case-validity rules;
- arm assignment semantics;
- budget 13;
- frozen replay costs;
- C0-C3 arm semantics;
- primary effectiveness metric;
- safety metrics;
- resource metrics;
- denominator rules;
- invalid-execution rules;
- rerun rules;
- no-human-correction rule;
- H1 support criterion;
- safety criterion;
- stop conditions;
- result-retention rule;
- and final scientific claim boundary.

No protected case may exist before this protocol is frozen.

---

## Remaining gate 2 — protected sample-size and support criteria

The protected master protocol must preregister numeric sample counts and numeric
support criteria before protected outcomes exist.

At minimum it must freeze:

- total authorized in-RFS denominator;
- authorized count for each in-RFS fault stratum;
- authorized out-of-RFS safety count;
- the exact C3-versus-C2 effectiveness support rule;
- the exact false-specific safety rule;
- treatment of ties;
- treatment of budget exhaustion;
- treatment of `INCONCLUSIVE`;
- treatment of infrastructure-invalid runs;
- and whether any descriptive resource metric has claim authority.

Development outcomes must not be searched to choose a threshold that favors C3.

---

## Remaining gate 3 — protected generator and evaluator implementation

Before protected case generation, implement and development-test the protected
study machinery.

The protected generator must separate:

- public treatment-visible case material;
- hidden evaluator truth;
- generation provenance;
- and immutable manifests.

The evaluator must receive hidden truth only after treatment outputs are frozen.

Treatment code must not import or read evaluator truth.

The evaluator must not modify treatment records.

This gate is implementation qualification only.

---

## Remaining gate 4 — protected C1 seed commitment

Only after the master protocol and protected-study machinery are frozen may the
protected C1 randomization seed be generated.

The protected seed gate must:

- obtain exactly 32 bytes from the operating-system cryptographic random
  source;
- occur exactly once;
- freeze a public SHA-256 commitment;
- preserve raw seed bytes only inside the authorized execution evidence
  boundary;
- prohibit retry or replacement;
- and occur before protected treatment behavior is observed.

The protected C1 seed must not reuse `bytes(range(32))`.

The development seed has no protected authority.

---

## Remaining gate 5 — one-shot protected population generation

After the master protocol, generator qualification, and required randomness
commitments are frozen, generate the protected population exactly once.

The generation gate must freeze:

- public case manifest;
- hidden evaluator manifest;
- generation record;
- generation disposition;
- generation code SHA-256;
- protocol SHA-256;
- source checkpoint;
- and any preregistered generation seed commitment.

No treatment arm may execute before the protected population is frozen.

Individual hidden case-to-fault mappings must not be exposed to treatment
execution.

---

## Remaining gate 6 — protected execution authorization

After population freeze and before treatment execution, perform an explicit
authorization gate.

It must verify:

- exact repository checkpoint;
- exact protected protocol;
- exact scientific-arm implementation;
- exact budget and replay costs;
- exact population manifests;
- exact C1 seed commitment;
- hidden-truth isolation;
- evaluator separation;
- regression status;
- rerun policy;
- execution environment;
- and absence of premature treatment artifacts.

Authorization must be a separate recorded decision.

Population existence alone does not authorize execution.

---

## Remaining gate 7 — one-shot protected treatment execution

Protected treatment must run exactly under the frozen protocol.

The execution record must retain all authorized cases and arm outcomes.

Budget exhaustion, inconclusive outcomes, wrong diagnoses, and unfavorable C3
results remain part of the frozen evidence.

There is no result-dependent retry.

Any preregistered infrastructure-invalid execution is handled only by the
already-frozen invalid/rerun rule.

---

## Remaining gate 8 — separated protected evaluation

After treatment outputs are frozen, run the evaluator against hidden truth.

The evaluator must compute only preregistered metrics and support rules.

It must not change:

- treatment selections;
- observations;
- diagnoses;
- abstentions;
- budget outcomes;
- denominators;
- or thresholds.

Evaluation output must be separately fingerprinted and frozen.

---

## Remaining gate 9 — interpretation and result freeze

Only after protected execution and evaluation are frozen may the P09.4
scientific result be interpreted.

Interpretation must distinguish:

- observed protected outcomes;
- preregistered support criteria;
- whether H1 is supported, not supported, or blocked;
- mechanism evidence;
- safety evidence;
- resource observations;
- and external-validity limitations.

A negative result must be retained.

A mixed result must be retained.

A safety failure must be retained.

No post-hoc redesign is permitted to recover a preferred conclusion.

---

## Order constraint

The protected sequence is strictly ordered:

1. master protected protocol;
2. sample-size/support criteria within that protocol;
3. protected generator/evaluator implementation and qualification;
4. protected C1 seed commitment;
5. one-shot protected population generation and freeze;
6. protected execution authorization;
7. one-shot protected treatment execution;
8. separated protected evaluation;
9. interpretation and result freeze.

A later stage must not occur before every required earlier gate is frozen.
