## 3. Source-grounded scientific constraints

The frozen protocol requires C1 and population-generation seeds to
be different 256-bit values from separate OS cryptographic
random-source calls.

Their SHA-256 commitments must differ. A commitment collision or
identical raw seeds is an integrity failure that blocks execution.

C1 is intended to reuse its single protected raw seed across all
protected C1 cases. Frozen case identity and selection sequence
provide domain separation; rerolling the seed for an inconvenient
selection is not permitted.

K5D precedes K5E. Therefore a real K5D C1 commitment, even if
separately authorized and created, cannot alone establish later
two-seed independence. The second protected seed and its provenance
must be established during separately authorized K5E work, followed
by required checks before protected execution.

Different commitment values alone do not prove that separate
OS-random calls actually occurred.

## 4. Required engineering design decisions

The following are unresolved design and control questions, not
already-approved scientific procedures.

### D1. Authorization and execution separation

Specify who may approve the irreversible real C1 operation, what
evidence records that approval, what exact identity is approved,
and how execution refuses operation without it.

The development API's caller-supplied seed factory is not sufficient
as protected authorization or randomness-provenance enforcement.

### D2. Protected artifact and identity schema

Approve the canonical protected C1 identity, its location, public
commitment schema, private raw-seed schema, lineage identifiers,
permissions, and immutable disposition states.

The K5D-3 temporary fixture paths are illustrative and must not
silently become the protected production schema.

### D3. Irreversible lifecycle and recovery

Define preflight, identity claim, STARTED, successful creation,
durable commitment, failed/incomplete attempts, and terminal
disposition behavior.

A claimed identity must not be silently deleted, retried, or
regenerated to obtain a preferred result.

File visibility and confirmed durability are different conditions.
Uncertain durability must not be treated as permission to reroll.

### D4. File-system trust and threat model

Specify the authorized filesystem, ownership and access
assumptions, path-confinement strategy, symlink/race controls,
concurrency rules, private-data handling, and crash recovery.

The current development implementation is not qualified against
adversarial concurrent filesystem manipulation.

### D5. OS-random provenance and non-disclosure

Specify how a protected operation obtains exactly 32 bytes using
an OS cryptographic random source, without revealing raw material
in logs, exceptions, public artifacts, or diagnostic outputs.

The approval should bind provenance to the real one-shot operation,
not merely to a synthetic test result.

### D6. Frozen lineage and downstream checks

Bind the approved operation to the exact frozen protocol and
approved protected schema.

Preserve sufficient evidence for later K5E independence checks
without exposing raw C1 seed material outside the authorized
treatment-execution boundary.

## 5. Readiness gates

**Gate R1 — This gap inventory**

Source-grounded gaps are recorded. No production design or protected
authorization is granted.

**Gate R2 — Protected-design review**

An independently reviewed engineering contract resolves D1-D6 and
defines acceptance tests. It remains a design qualification.

**Gate R3 — Development-only implementation qualification**

Any new machinery is tested using synthetic fixtures and isolated
locations, with fresh regression, fault-injection, preservation,
and evidence review. Development qualification remains distinct
from protected authorization.

**Gate R4 — Explicit protected K5D authorization**

A separately approved, enforceable one-shot operation is required
before any real C1 seed is created. This inventory cannot grant
that authorization.

**Gate R5 — Future K5E authorization and generation**

Only after its separate authorization may K5E create a distinct
generation seed and protected population.

**Gate R6 — Later independence and execution checks**

After both real seed commitments exist, check the frozen
independence requirements before any separately authorized
protected treatment execution.

Passing an earlier gate does not implicitly authorize a later one.
