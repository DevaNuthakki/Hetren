# P09.4 C2-versus-C3 Policy-Separability Audit Protocol

## Status

**DEVELOPMENT-ONLY MECHANISM PROTOCOL — NO SCIENTIFIC PERFORMANCE AUTHORITY**

This protocol freezes the audit used to determine whether the calibrated P09.4
replay-cost catalogue can cause C2 and C3 to select different diagnostic
experiments on valid reachable development states.

This step does not execute the audit.

No C0-C3 outcome comparison is authorized.

No protected P09.4 generation or execution is authorized.

No final scientific budget is selected.

No H1 support or C3 superiority conclusion is authorized.

---

## Audit question

The sole primary question is:

**Given exactly the same valid reachable diagnosis state, can C2
max-information-gain and C3 information-gain-per-cost select different
experiments because of the frozen `{4,4,3,3,3}` replay-cost catalogue?**

This is a mechanism-identification question.

It is not an accuracy question.

It is not a treatment-performance question.

---

## Frozen cost catalogue

The development-only calibrated replay costs are exactly:

- P1 `p1-canonical-tool` = 4;
- P2 `p2-canonical-parameter` = 4;
- P3 `p3-canonical-prerequisite-order` = 3;
- P4 `p4-fresh-context` = 3;
- P5 `p5-environment-contract` = 3.

No cost may be tuned during the audit.

`B_full = 17` remains a mechanical full-affordability landmark.

It is not the final P09.4 scientific evaluation budget.

---

## Historical problem preservation

The historical `build_development_problem` function remains unchanged.

The audit must construct an additive in-memory calibrated-cost
`DiagnosisProblem`.

The calibrated audit problem must preserve exactly:

- the historical trace;
- the historical hypotheses;
- hypothesis descriptions;
- hypothesis priors;
- experiment keys;
- intervention kinds;
- intervention targets;
- predicted verdict tables;
- experiment ordering.

Only experiment `cost_units` and the audit budget may differ in the additive
audit problem.

No tracked source code is modified merely to perform the audit.

---

## Calibrated audit problem

The primary audit problem uses:

`budget_units = B_full = 17`

and the frozen `{4,4,3,3,3}` experiment costs.

The purpose of using `B_full` is full experiment affordability rather than final
budget selection.

At any history formed by executing distinct experiments with their frozen
costs, the remaining budget equals the cost of the unexecuted catalogue.

Therefore every remaining experiment is individually affordable in the primary
full-affordability state audit.

This prevents budget exclusion from masquerading as the primary C2/C3 ranking
difference.

---

## Equal-cost negative control

The audit must build a second in-memory problem with the same trace,
hypotheses, experiment definitions, ordering, and deterministic verdict
semantics, but with every experiment cost set to 1.

The equal-cost control uses:

`budget_units = 5`

so every unexecuted experiment remains affordable after every valid history.

Under equal costs, C3's information-gain-per-cost ordering should reduce to raw
information-gain ordering subject to the same deterministic tie-breaking.

The equal-cost control must therefore be checked across the entire primary
reachable active-state set, not only at one hand-selected state.

If C2 and equal-cost C3 diverge on a state for a reason unrelated to costs, the
audit must stop and identify that mechanism rather than attributing divergence
to calibrated replay cost.

---

## Reachability definition

A reachable state is generated from the frozen historical five-hypothesis
problem using only its registered deterministic experiment prediction tables.

The audit starts from the historical initial prior.

A successor state may be created only by:

1. selecting an experiment that has not appeared earlier in that history;
2. taking PASS or FAIL only when that verdict has positive posterior
   probability under the experiment's registered prediction table;
3. applying the existing `posterior_after_observation` function;
4. preserving exact posterior normalization;
5. recording the experiment as consumed;
6. charging the frozen audit cost for that experiment.

No arbitrary posterior vector may be inserted.

No hand-authored hypothesis probability may be inserted.

No evaluator truth or latent fault label may be used to create reachable
states.

---

## Exhaustive state enumeration

The audit must enumerate the reachable deterministic state graph rather than
selecting a favorable example.

State exploration begins at the initial prior and recursively considers every
remaining experiment and every supported PASS/FAIL outcome.

A state signature must contain at least:

- posterior probabilities in canonical hypothesis-key order;
- consumed experiment keys;
- accumulated calibrated cost;
- remaining calibrated budget.

Equivalent states reached by different histories may be reported separately
for history coverage, but the audit must also report the number of unique state
signatures.

---

## Active-state scope

Primary C2/C3 ranking comparison applies only when:

- at least two hypotheses have nonzero posterior probability;
- at least one unexecuted experiment remains;
- at least one remaining experiment has positive expected information gain;
- and the primary `B_full` audit budget makes the experiment eligible.

A singleton posterior is not treated as a ranking opportunity.

Singleton states are recorded separately for open-set confirmation coverage.

---

## C2 rule

For each primary active state, C2 ranks eligible remaining experiments by:

1. raw expected information gain descending;
2. experiment key ascending.

Expected information gain must come from the existing
`expected_information_gain` implementation.

No cost term may enter the C2 ranking.

---

## C3 rule

For the same primary active state, C3 ranks eligible remaining experiments
using the existing planner ordering:

1. information gain divided by frozen experiment cost descending;
2. raw information gain descending;
3. cost ascending;
4. experiment key ascending.

The audit should use the existing `rank_eligible_experiments` implementation
against the additive calibrated-cost problem.

A separate handwritten C3 ranking implementation must not replace the existing
planner for the primary result.

---

## Same-state fairness

For every compared state, C2 and C3 must receive exactly the same:

- trace;
- posterior;
- consumed experiment set;
- remaining experiment set;
- remaining budget;
- candidate eligibility;
- hypothesis definitions;
- prediction tables.

The only intended difference is the ranking objective.

C2 uses raw information gain.

C3 uses information gain divided by frozen replay cost.

---

## Required per-state record

For every primary active state, record:

- deterministic state ID;
- generating observation-history descriptor;
- posterior;
- consumed experiment keys;
- remaining calibrated budget;
- eligible experiment keys;
- each eligible experiment's raw information gain;
- each eligible experiment's calibrated replay cost;
- each eligible experiment's information-gain-per-cost score;
- C2 ranking;
- C3 ranking;
- C2 selected experiment;
- C3 selected experiment;
- whether selections diverge.

The state record must be sufficient to explain every divergence without
consulting evaluator truth.

---

## Primary policy-separability criterion

Policy separability is established in deterministic development only if at
least one valid reachable primary active state has:

`C2 selected experiment != C3 selected experiment`

while all same-state fairness conditions hold.

The result must report every divergent reachable state found by exhaustive
enumeration.

The result must not report only the first divergence.

---

## Cost-attribution criterion

A primary divergence counts as cost-attributable only if, on the same reachable
posterior and consumed-experiment state, the equal-cost C3 control selects the
same experiment as C2.

Formally, for a cost-attributable divergent state:

- calibrated C2 selection differs from calibrated C3 selection;
- equal-cost C3 selection equals C2 selection.

If this pattern is not observed, calibrated replay cost has not isolated the
selection difference.

---

## Equal-cost global negative-control criterion

Across all corresponding active reachable states, equal-cost C3 must match C2.

Any equal-cost mismatch must be retained and investigated.

The audit must not silently discard mismatching states.

A failed equal-cost control blocks a clean cost-attribution conclusion.

---

## Initial state

The initial prior is automatically part of exhaustive reachability and must be
reported.

It must not receive special weighting.

If the initial state separates C2 and C3, it is valid evidence because it was
included by the frozen exhaustive rule rather than selected after inspection.

If it does not separate them, that is also retained.

---

## Longer reachable histories

The audit must include active reachable states after one or more supported
observations whenever more than one hypothesis remains viable.

The audit therefore tests whether any divergence is isolated to the initial
prior or persists, appears, or disappears later in the diagnostic process.

---

## Singleton and open-set confirmation coverage

When a valid history leaves one RFS hypothesis with posterior probability 1,
the state is recorded as a singleton state rather than an ordinary C2/C3
ranking state.

The audit must identify the corresponding remaining confirming experiment when
available.

It must confirm that the calibrated cost assigned to that confirmation is the
same ordinary frozen replay cost used elsewhere.

No free confirmation is permitted.

The existing open-set implementation and its regression tests remain unchanged.

---

## Out-of-RFS boundary

The constructed out-of-RFS report-assembly control is not used to manufacture
a posterior for primary C2/C3 ranking.

Its role in this gate is safety regression only.

Existing evidence must continue to show:

- P1-P5 all FAIL;
- contradiction is detected;
- terminal status is `INCONCLUSIVE`;
- no false-specific diagnosis is emitted.

Policy separability does not override this safety requirement.

---

## Budget-eligibility secondary audit

The primary separability audit uses full affordability at `B_full = 17`.

A separate secondary eligibility audit must evaluate only the already-derived
mechanical budget landmarks:

- 3;
- 6;
- 9;
- 13;
- 17.

These landmarks are development audit inputs, not candidate final scientific
budgets.

For each landmark, the audit records the initial eligible experiment set under
the calibrated cost catalogue.

This secondary table is descriptive.

A C2/C3 divergence caused solely because one policy was given a different
eligible set is not valid policy-separability evidence.

Both policies must always receive the same eligibility set at a given
landmark.

---

## Determinism requirement

The complete reachable-state enumeration and all per-state ranking records must
be generated twice.

Primary and repeat structured records must be exactly equal.

A deterministic-repeat mismatch invalidates the audit.

The repeat is not averaged.

---

## No outcome-performance metrics

A2C6I2 must not calculate or claim:

- diagnosis accuracy;
- false-specific rate as a treatment comparison;
- C2 success rate;
- C3 success rate;
- diagnosis cost advantage;
- H1 support;
- treatment effect;
- superiority;
- production benefit.

This audit concerns selection mechanics only.

---

## Negative-result rule

If exhaustive valid reachable-state enumeration finds no cost-attributable
C2/C3 divergence, the result is negative.

That negative result must be retained.

Costs must not be redesigned merely to force divergence without a new reviewed
development rationale.

---

## Positive-result boundary

If at least one valid reachable state demonstrates cost-attributable divergence
and the equal-cost global control passes, the only permitted conclusion is:

**C2 and C3 are policy-separable under the frozen deterministic development
problem and calibrated replay-cost catalogue.**

This does not mean C3 is more accurate.

This does not mean C3 is cheaper overall.

This does not mean C3 is scientifically superior.

---

## Historical preservation requirements

The audit must not modify:

- `build_development_problem`;
- historical planner code;
- historical lifecycle code;
- open-set code;
- A2C6E replay implementation;
- A2C6F calibration protocol;
- A2C6G calibration result;
- A2C6H review;
- P09.3 protected evidence.

The preferred execution is a read-only analytical audit that emits one result
document.

---

## Protected-work prohibition

A2C6I2 must not:

- generate a protected P09.4 population;
- execute protected P09.4 treatment arms;
- inspect future protected outcomes;
- select a final P09.4 budget;
- freeze final H1 support thresholds.

---

## Acceptance criteria

A2C6I2 passes as a policy-separability mechanism audit only if:

1. the frozen `{4,4,3,3,3}` catalogue is used exactly;
2. historical problem semantics are preserved;
3. primary active states are exhaustively reachable from registered prediction
   semantics;
4. arbitrary posterior fabrication is absent;
5. C2 and C3 use identical states and eligibility sets;
6. C2 uses raw information gain only;
7. C3 uses the existing information-gain-per-cost planner ranking;
8. every divergent reachable state is reported;
9. at least one divergence exists for a positive separability result;
10. equal-cost C3 equals C2 across the corresponding active-state graph;
11. every positive divergence is removed by the equal-cost control;
12. deterministic primary and repeat records are exact;
13. singleton/open-set confirmation states are accounted for separately;
14. out-of-RFS abstention regression remains preserved;
15. no outcome-performance comparison is performed;
16. no protected P09.4 work occurs;
17. repository regression remains green.

---

## Stop conditions

Stop without a positive policy-separability conclusion if:

- the reachable-state graph cannot be reproduced deterministically;
- C2/C3 candidate eligibility differs on the same state;
- equal-cost C3 differs from C2;
- a claimed divergence requires an impossible posterior;
- a claimed divergence requires evaluator truth;
- existing planner semantics must be altered merely to obtain divergence;
- or historical open-set safety regresses.

A stop or negative result is valid evidence.

---

## Scientific authority boundary

Before A2C6I2 executes, we may say only:

- the replay-cost catalogue is resource-non-degenerate;
- the cost-aware ranking mechanism is mechanically testable.

We may not yet say that C2 and C3 are policy-separable.

We may not say that C3 performs better than C2.

We may not say that Hetren reduces real-world debugging cost.
