## Why this catalogue is acceptable for further development audit

The model passes the development calibration requirements because:

- costs came from actual replay `TOOL_CALL` counts;
- the common already-executed failed baseline was excluded consistently;
- each probe used the same replay cost across all seven frozen case classes;
- PASS and FAIL did not alter a probe's price;
- latent fault identity did not alter a probe's price;
- no no-op padding was introduced;
- no post-hoc workflow-step weighting was introduced;
- primary and repeat calibration records were exact;
- and historical development cost fields were not rewritten.

The cost difference is therefore structural in the frozen replay executor.

---

## Structural source of cost difference

P1 and P2 replay from the pre-filter boundary.

Their replay path performs:

- severity filtering;
- enrichment;
- validation;
- report construction.

That produces four replay tool interactions.

P3 and P5 replay from the post-filter boundary.

Their replay path performs:

- enrichment;
- validation;
- report construction.

That produces three replay tool interactions.

P4 replays from the pre-validation boundary while refreshing context.

Its replay path performs:

- context refresh;
- validation;
- report construction.

That also produces three replay tool interactions.

This review treats these counts as the calibrated deterministic development
replay-cost model because A2C6F froze the measurement rule before the dedicated
A2C6G matrix and deterministic repeat.

## Resource non-degeneracy conclusion

The catalogue is resource-non-degenerate because at least two probes have
different calibrated replay costs.

P1 and P2 cost 4 development replay units.

P3, P4, and P5 cost 3 development replay units.

This makes information-gain-per-cost mathematically capable of ranking
experiments differently from raw information gain.

That is a necessary condition for the intended C3 mechanism.

It is not sufficient evidence that C3 behaves differently from C2 on the
reachable diagnosis states.

---

## New distinction: resource non-degeneracy versus policy separability

Three separate claims must remain distinct.

### 1. Resource non-degeneracy

At least two registered experiments have different frozen costs.

A2C6G establishes this in deterministic development.

### 2. Policy separability

Given the same reachable diagnosis state and the same eligible experiments,
C2 max-information-gain and C3 information-gain-per-cost select different
experiments for at least one state because of the frozen cost catalogue.

This has not yet been established.

### 3. Scientific superiority

Under a preregistered fair evaluation, C3 produces better bounded outcomes than
the comparison arm.

This has not been tested.

Resource non-degeneracy must not be reported as either policy separability or
scientific superiority.

---

## Why policy separability must be tested before C0-C3

Different experiment prices do not guarantee different planner behavior.

For example, raw information gain may be sufficiently larger for an expensive
experiment that both C2 and C3 still select it.

Alternatively, ties and deterministic key ordering may cause C2 and C3 to
choose differently even when the meaningful scoring signal is otherwise weak.

Therefore the next development gate must inspect the actual planner scoring and
selection behavior over reachable diagnosis states.

A C2-versus-C3 scientific comparison should not be preregistered until we know
that the two policies are behaviorally separable for the intended mechanism.

---

## Reachable-state requirement for the next audit

The next audit must not manufacture arbitrary posterior vectors solely to force
C2/C3 divergence.

It should derive states from the frozen five-hypothesis diagnosis problem and
valid observation histories generated by the existing experiment prediction
semantics.

At minimum it must include:

- the initial prior state;
- reachable states after one valid observation;
- reachable states after longer valid observation histories while multiple
  hypotheses remain viable;
- states requiring open-set unique confirmation;
- and any state where budget eligibility changes the candidate experiment set.

Impossible or internally contradictory synthetic posterior states must not be
used as evidence of policy separability.

---

## Same-state fairness rule

When C2 and C3 are compared for policy separability, both policies must receive:

- the same trace;
- the same hypothesis priors;
- the same observation history;
- the same remaining experiments;
- the same candidate eligibility rules;
- the same budget state;
- and the same deterministic tie-breaking contract except for the intended
  score difference.

The only intended ranking difference is:

- C2: information gain;
- C3: information gain divided by frozen calibrated replay cost.

## Open-set boundary

Policy separability must not weaken open-set safety.

A policy difference is scientifically irrelevant if it is achieved by bypassing
confirmation or allowing false-specific diagnosis.

The next audit therefore must retain:

- contradiction-aware replay;
- RFS-unique confirmation;
- ordinary cost for confirmation;
- `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` when required confirmation is unaffordable;
- and `INCONCLUSIVE` for the constructed out-of-RFS contradiction case.

## No performance metric in the next audit

The next audit must not report diagnosis accuracy, false-specific rate,
treatment superiority, or H1 support.

Its question is purely mechanistic:

**Can the frozen C2 and C3 ranking rules choose differently on at least one
valid reachable development state, with the difference attributable to the
frozen replay-cost catalogue?**

---

## Acceptance criteria for policy separability

A future development policy-separability result may pass only if:

1. C2 and C3 receive the exact same reachable diagnosis state.
2. C2 ranks by raw information gain.
3. C3 ranks by information gain divided by frozen replay cost.
4. The cost catalogue is exactly `{P1:4, P2:4, P3:3, P4:3, P5:3}`.
5. At least one reachable state yields different selected experiments.
6. The divergence is reproducible and deterministic.
7. Removing cost asymmetry or setting all costs equal removes the
   cost-attributable divergence where expected.
8. Historical planner/core behavior remains preserved.
9. No C0-C3 outcome comparison is performed.
10. No protected P09.4 population or execution is touched.

If no valid reachable state separates C2 and C3, the negative result must be
retained.

---

## Recommended negative control

The policy-separability audit should include an equal-cost control.

Under that control, every experiment receives the same candidate cost.

The equal-cost control is intended to demonstrate that any C2/C3 difference
attributed to cost disappears when cost asymmetry is removed, subject to the
same tie-breaking semantics.

If a difference persists for reasons unrelated to cost, the audit must identify
that mechanism rather than crediting calibrated cost.
