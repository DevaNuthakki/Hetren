# P09.4 Scientific C0-C3 Arm Semantics and Budget-Selection Preregistration

## Status

**PRE-BUDGET SCIENTIFIC-DESIGN PREREGISTRATION — NO EXECUTION AUTHORITY**

This document freezes the treatment semantics and mechanical budget-selection
procedure that must govern the next P09.4 scientific-design steps.

It does not select the final scientific budget.

It does not execute C0, C1, C2, or C3.

It does not generate a protected P09.4 population.

It does not authorize protected execution.

It does not establish H1 support.

---

## Scientific-design objective

The future controlled comparison is intended to test whether cost-aware active
experiment selection provides a bounded diagnosis advantage under a shared,
preregistered resource budget while preserving open-set safety.

The principal comparison is C3 versus C2.

C1 and C0 provide progressively weaker selection-policy comparators.

No arm result has scientific authority until the later protected protocol is
fully frozen and executed.

---

## Frozen diagnosis resource model

The candidate replay-cost catalogue is exactly:

- P1 `p1-canonical-tool` = 4 units;
- P2 `p2-canonical-parameter` = 4 units;
- P3 `p3-canonical-prerequisite-order` = 3 units;
- P4 `p4-fresh-context` = 3 units;
- P5 `p5-environment-contract` = 3 units.

These units represent newly executed replay `TOOL_CALL` interactions after the
common failed baseline.

They do not represent dollars, wall-clock time, GPU cost, CPU cost, or
production latency.

`B_full = 17` is the cost of executing every registered replay experiment once.

---

## Shared hypothesis model

Every scientific treatment arm must receive the same registered five-hypothesis
RFS problem for a given case.

The hypothesis catalogue, priors, descriptions, experiment predictions, and
trace visible to one treatment arm must be identical to those visible to the
other treatment arms for that case.

Treatment arms must not receive evaluator truth.

Treatment arms must not receive the latent fault label.

A future population protocol may vary treatment-visible case observations, but
must not vary arm-visible evidence within the same case.

---

## Shared evidence rule

Planner prediction tables may be used to score candidate experiments.

They may not be substituted for execution evidence.

Whenever an active arm selects an experiment, its observation must come from
the actual frozen checkpoint/resume replay executor.

The observed verdict must be bound to the executed replay evidence.

No planner may fabricate a PASS or FAIL observation from its own prediction
table.

---

## Shared experiment-consumption rule

A diagnostic experiment may be executed at most once per case and arm.

After execution it is consumed and removed from later candidate sets.

The experiment's ordinary frozen replay cost is charged exactly once.

Repeated execution for the purpose of changing a result is prohibited.

---

## Shared budget accounting

C0-C3 receive the same nominal scientific budget cap once that budget is
selected.

For active arms, every executed diagnostic experiment consumes its frozen
calibrated replay cost.

Realized treatment cost is the sum of executed experiment costs.

Declared and actually executed replay interaction counts must agree.

A mismatch between declared calibrated cost and replay execution accounting is
an execution-integrity failure, not a reason to silently alter the price.

---

## C0 — trace-only comparator

C0 executes no active diagnostic experiment.

C0 consumes zero active replay-cost units.

C0 does not manufacture a specific diagnosis from untested RFS hypotheses.

Its trace-only terminal outcome is retained in the denominator.

C0 exists to separate active diagnosis from passive trace observation.

---

## C1 — seeded-random active comparator

C1 uses the same eligible experiment set, budget accounting, observation
execution, posterior update, open-set logic, and terminal rules as C2 and C3.

Its only intended policy difference is experiment selection.

At each ordinary active-selection step:

1. construct the currently eligible experiment keys;
2. sort the keys lexicographically;
3. choose one key using the frozen deterministic seeded-randomization function.

C1 samples without replacement because executed experiments are consumed.

C1 must not use information gain or latent truth to select an experiment.

---

## C1 seed commitment procedure

The C1 raw seed is not chosen in this step.

Before protected P09.4 case generation or treatment execution, a dedicated
one-shot gate must:

1. obtain exactly 32 random bytes from the operating-system cryptographic
   random source;
2. freeze `SHA-256(raw_seed_bytes)` as the public seed commitment;
3. preserve the raw seed in the authorized execution evidence boundary;
4. prohibit regeneration after commitment.

No seed may be discarded because its later treatment sequence is inconvenient.

The seed must be committed before protected outcomes exist.

---

## C1 deterministic selection function

For a case and active-selection sequence, C1 must compute a deterministic
selection digest from:

- the committed raw seed bytes;
- case ID;
- zero-based active-selection sequence;
- the lexicographically sorted eligible experiment keys.

The serialization contract must be frozen before protected execution.

The digest is interpreted as a non-negative integer.

The selected index is:

`digest_integer mod number_of_eligible_experiments`

The selected experiment is the key at that index in the sorted eligible list.

The same seed and same state must always produce the same choice.

---

## C2 — maximum-information-gain comparator

C2 selects among the same currently eligible experiments available to C3.

C2 ranking is frozen as:

1. expected information gain descending;
2. experiment key ascending.

Calibrated replay cost affects C2 eligibility through the remaining budget.

Calibrated replay cost must not enter C2's ranking score.

---

## C3 — cost-aware active policy

C3 selects among the same currently eligible experiments available to C2.

C3 ranking is frozen as:

1. expected information gain divided by calibrated replay cost descending;
2. raw expected information gain descending;
3. replay cost ascending;
4. experiment key ascending.

No other cost weighting may be introduced after protected outcomes are
observed.

---

## C2/C3 same-state fairness

For any comparable case state, C2 and C3 must receive the same:

- treatment-visible trace;
- RFS hypotheses;
- priors;
- observation history before selection;
- remaining experiment catalogue;
- experiment prediction tables;
- remaining budget;
- eligibility rule;
- confirmation rule;
- posterior-update rule.

Their intended policy difference is the ranking objective.

A difference caused by different evidence or different candidate sets must not
be credited to cost-aware selection.

---

## Shared posterior update

C1-C3 use the same deterministic posterior-update implementation.

Observations are replayed in contiguous sequence.

An experiment cannot appear twice.

Posterior state is derived only from prior probability and registered
experiment predictions conditioned on actual observed verdicts.

Evaluator truth is not an input.

---

## Shared open-set lifecycle

C1-C3 must use the same frozen open-set lifecycle.

A specific diagnosis requires RFS-unique confirmation.

Evidence contradiction must not be converted into a specific diagnosis merely
because the closed RFS has one remaining convenient label.

The out-of-RFS safety path remains capable of terminating `INCONCLUSIVE`.

---

## Confirmation accounting

Mandatory confirmation is not free.

If a specific diagnosis requires a confirming experiment that has not yet been
executed, that experiment consumes its ordinary frozen calibrated replay cost.

If the remaining budget cannot afford required confirmation, the terminal
status is `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

A treatment arm may not exceed the shared budget to obtain confirmation.

---

## Budget-exhaustion semantics

Budget exhaustion is an observable treatment outcome.

It must remain in the scientific denominator.

It must not be silently converted to `INCONCLUSIVE`, specific diagnosis, or
missing data.

No arm receives extra budget because an observed case is difficult.

---

## Inconclusive semantics

`INCONCLUSIVE` is an explicit observable outcome.

It remains in the appropriate scientific denominator.

Abstention must not be removed simply because it reduces an arm's apparent
success rate.

---

## Out-of-RFS safety stratum

A future protected population must contain a preregistered out-of-RFS safety
stratum before scientific execution.

For an out-of-RFS case:

- any emitted RFS-specific diagnosis is false-specific;
- contradiction-aware `INCONCLUSIVE` is a valid abstention outcome;
- budget exhaustion remains budget exhaustion;
- the case remains in the safety denominator.

The out-of-RFS stratum is not included in the in-RFS correct-specific
effectiveness denominator.

---

## In-RFS effectiveness stratum

The primary effectiveness denominator contains every authorized in-RFS
protected case assigned to the evaluated arm.

Correct-specific diagnosis counts as correct.

False-specific diagnosis does not count as correct.

`INCONCLUSIVE` does not count as correct.

`BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` does not count as correct.

Treatment-level execution failure does not count as correct.

No unsuccessful in-RFS case may disappear from the denominator.

---

## Primary effectiveness metric

The primary scientific effectiveness metric is:

`correct-specific in-RFS cases / all authorized in-RFS cases`

The principal future treatment contrast is C3 versus C2.

Exact support thresholds and protected sample sizes are not frozen by A2C6K1.

They require a later preregistration gate before protected execution.

---

## Safety metrics

The later scientific evaluation must report at minimum:

- false-specific rate over all evaluated cases;
- false-specific rate in the out-of-RFS safety stratum;
- out-of-RFS abstention rate;
- `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` rate;
- `INCONCLUSIVE` rate.

Safety results must be reported even if the primary effectiveness comparison is
positive.

---

## Resource metrics

The later scientific evaluation must report for every arm:

- realized calibrated replay cost per case;
- number of executed diagnostic experiments per case;
- terminal remaining budget;
- terminal outcome category.

These are descriptive resource outcomes unless a later preregistration assigns
a specific support criterion.

No real-world monetary, latency, or compute claim follows from replay-cost
units.

---

## Denominator immutability

Every authorized protected case has a predefined stratum and arm assignment.

After treatment begins, a case may not be removed because it:

- is difficult;
- exhausts budget;
- becomes inconclusive;
- produces an unfavorable arm result;
- or fails to produce a specific diagnosis.

Any infrastructure-level validity rule must be frozen before protected
execution.

No post-hoc denominator repair is permitted.

---

## No human correction

No human may change:

- an arm's selected experiment;
- an observed verdict;
- a posterior;
- a diagnosis;
- an abstention;
- or a budget outcome

after observing treatment behavior.

If an implementation-integrity failure occurs, the frozen stop/rerun protocol
must govern it.

---

## Rerun boundary

There is no automatic case-level rerun for an unfavorable scientific outcome.

A scientific case is not rerun because an arm exhausts budget, abstains, or
makes a wrong diagnosis.

A later final execution protocol must distinguish treatment outcomes from
predefined infrastructure-invalid executions before any protected run occurs.

No rerun rule may be invented after protected outcomes are seen.

---

## Candidate budget set

The only candidate scientific budget values considered by the next
budget-selection gate are:

- 3;
- 6;
- 9;
- 13;
- 17.

No intermediate value may be introduced after inspecting treatment
performance.

`17` is the full-catalogue affordability landmark.

---

## Budget-selection principle

The scientific budget must be selected from resource mechanics only.

The selection process must not execute C0-C3 diagnosis-performance comparisons.

It must not use:

- correct-diagnosis rate;
- false-specific treatment rate;
- C2/C3 outcome differences;
- hidden fault labels;
- protected outcomes;
- or which budget makes C3 look strongest.

The budget-selection result is therefore a design result, not a treatment
result.

---

## Budget initial-affordability requirement

A candidate sub-full budget is mechanically admissible only if every registered
experiment is individually affordable at the initial state.

Therefore an admissible candidate must be at least the maximum single
experiment cost.

This protects the initial candidate catalogue from excluding an RFS hypothesis
solely because its confirming probe is unaffordable before any policy decision.

---

## Binding-budget requirement

The preferred scientific budget is strictly less than `B_full`.

This keeps the resource constraint capable of affecting sequential selection.

A full budget is used only as a fallback if no sub-full candidate satisfies the
frozen mechanical admissibility rules.

---

## Minimally restrictive selection rule

Among admissible candidate budgets strictly below `B_full`, select the
numerically largest candidate.

This is the frozen budget-selection rule.

Its rationale is to preserve the maximum resource flexibility possible while
retaining a genuinely binding budget.

It minimizes avoidable resource starvation without allowing complete
five-probe enumeration.

A2C6K1 does not apply this rule or declare its resulting budget.

---

## Full-budget fallback

If no candidate below `B_full` satisfies initial affordability, select
`B_full`.

The fallback must be recorded as a mechanical design consequence.

A different budget may not be substituted because of treatment-performance
expectations.

---

## Budget-selection audit requirements

The next gate must mechanically report for each candidate budget:

- whether all five probes are individually initially affordable;
- whether the budget is sub-full or full;
- which probes are initially eligible;
- the maximum single-probe cost;
- whether the candidate satisfies the frozen admissibility rule.

The next gate then applies the minimally restrictive selection rule exactly
once.

It must not execute treatment outcomes.

---

## C0 budget treatment

C0 receives the same nominal selected scientific budget as the active arms for
case-level design parity.

C0 performs no active replay experiments and therefore realizes zero active
replay cost.

Unused C0 budget is not transferred to another arm.

---

## Arm budget parity

C1, C2, and C3 receive identical nominal budget units on the same case.

No active arm receives a larger cap because its policy selects more expensive
experiments.

Budget parity is separate from realized-cost equality.

Realized costs may differ because policies may select different experiments or
terminate at different times.

---

## Final support criteria boundary

A2C6K1 freezes outcome definitions but does not freeze the numeric H1 support
threshold.

A later preregistration must define the support criterion before protected
outcomes exist.

That later criterion must include the principal C3-versus-C2 effectiveness
contrast and an explicit false-specific safety bound.

No threshold may be chosen after inspecting protected results.

---

## Negative-result preservation

If the eventual scientific result shows no C3 advantage over C2, retain it.

If C3 is worse than C2, retain it.

If safety worsens, retain it.

If cost-aware ordering changes choices but does not improve scientific
outcomes, retain it.

Mechanism success does not guarantee treatment success.

---

## Stop conditions

Scientific execution remains blocked if any of the following is unresolved:

- final budget not frozen;
- C1 seed procedure not implemented and committed;
- C1 seed commitment not frozen;
- arm semantics not implemented exactly;
- declared replay costs differ from execution accounting;
- denominator rules not implemented;
- open-set safety regresses;
- protected population protocol not frozen;
- final H1 support criterion not frozen;
- protected-generation authority not explicitly granted.

A blocked gate is a valid engineering outcome.
